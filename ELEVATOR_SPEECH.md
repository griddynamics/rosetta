# Rosetta: Engineering Governance for AI Coding Agents

## The Problem

AI coding agents are powerful — but they break down at organizational scale.

Without a shared foundation, every engineer builds their own prompts and workflows in isolation. Institutional knowledge stays locked in the heads of senior engineers. Compliance constraints, architectural standards, and hard-won lessons never reach the agents.

The result: agents that optimize for speed over correctness, and organizations where enforcing consistent standards across dozens or hundreds of developers becomes practically impossible.

---

## The Solution

**Rosetta** is an open-source governance and context layer that brings engineering discipline to AI coding agents.

It's not another proprietary agent. Rosetta works alongside the tools your engineers already use — Claude Code, Cursor, GitHub Copilot, and others — acting as a shared intelligence layer compiled into every agent consistently.

Rosetta gives agents persistent engineering judgment through five mechanisms:

| Mechanism | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **Rules** | Always-on standards, enforced automatically |
| **Skills** | Specialized expertise injected at the right moment |
| **Hooks** | Non-negotiable guardrails that cannot be bypassed |
| **Workflows** | Structured decision points that force agents to ask, not assume |
| **Subagents** | Peer review and validation built into the process |

Everything is versioned in Git and runs entirely within the your organization's security perimeter.

The core idea: **teach agents how to think, not just what to do.** Modern models already know Python, Java, and React. What they lack is your organization's engineering discipline.

---

## Proven in Production

Rosetta is live across multiple enterprise engagements today.

- **2× productivity gains or more** on brownfield projects once requirements are aligned — higher still on greenfield work
- Security, testing, and documentation are **enforced inside the workflow** — not left as optional steps

Enterprise AI engineering cannot run on ad-hoc prompts and individual heroics. It needs repeatability, guardrails, and shared standards that scale.
